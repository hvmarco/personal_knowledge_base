## Notes

- [Post by Tirthankar "TC" Chakraborty on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7478690057026207745/) — an argument that satellite land surface temperature should not be used to quantify urban heat hazard. LST is the bulk radiometric skin temperature of rooftops, canopy tops and asphalt, which by day run far hotter than the air people move through, so viral "55 °C in Madrid, 27 °C in a nearby forest" maps inflate perceived hazard. He adds that LST overstates the cooling benefit of urban trees, since satellites compare transpiring canopy tops against heated asphalt and never see the shaded ground, while the daytime *air* temperature reduction is an order of magnitude smaller and hyper-local. His stated worry is trust: when lived experience does not match what people assume 50 °C should feel like, confidence in the science erodes. Keywords: land surface temperature, urban heat island, air temperature, heat hazard, tree cooling, science communication. Related: [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Climate_Change]], [[Data]] #type/reference
    - Sources listed by the author in the comments:
        - Differences between urban heat signals for air temperature and LST: [10.1126/sciadv.abb9569](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb9569)
        - Mismatches between urban humid heat metrics and common LST-based patterns across scales: [10.1029/2022AV000729](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022AV000729)
        - Discrepancies in cooling efficiency of urban tree cover for LST versus air temperature: [10.1088/1748-9326/ad30a3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ad30a3)
        - Overestimation of income-based urban heat disparities when using LST: [10.1016/j.oneear.2023.05.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oneear.2023.05.016)
        - Comprehensive critique: [Satellite-derived Land Surface Temperatures Strongly Mischaracterise Urban Heat Hazard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16568) — Zhan, Bechtel, Du, Chakraborty, Kotthaus, Krayenhoff, Martilli, Naserikia, Nazarian, Roth, Sismanidis, Stewart & Voogt, preprint submitted 2025-09-20. Argues LST is frequently decoupled from human-relevant thermal impacts, especially by day, so using it for adaptation policy inflates estimated benefits.
    - Pushback in the thread worth keeping: Justine Kojo argued the fix is data declaration and stated limitations rather than abandoning LST, since these datasets are genuinely useful in observation-scarce regions when validated against ground data. Chakraborty replied that sparse in-city weather stations are by design (urban signals would contaminate broader climate records), that many research sensor networks do not follow meteorological siting protocols and carry daytime biases, and that city-scale patterns holding across many cities do not necessarily hold for any individual city — so he would be cautious using LST alone for city policy.
