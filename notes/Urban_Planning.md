## Notes

- [Post by Tirthankar "TC" Chakraborty on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7478690057026207745/): An argument that satellite-derived land surface temperature (LST) should not be used to quantify urban heat hazard. LST is the bulk radiometric "skin" temperature of whatever the satellite sees — rooftops, canopy tops, asphalt, parking lots — and during the day those surfaces run far hotter than the air people actually move through, so viral "55 C in Madrid; 27 C in a nearby forest" maps inflate perceived hazard. Chakraborty's specific claims: LST also overstates the cooling benefit of urban trees because satellites compare actively transpiring canopy tops against heated asphalt and never see the shaded ground underneath, while the daytime *air* temperature reduction is an order of magnitude smaller and hyper-local; and disclaimers like "this is surface temperature, but..." still anchor readers on the large number. His stated worry is trust: when lived experience does not match what people assume 50 C should feel like, confidence in the data and the science erodes. `land-surface-temperature` `urban-heat-island` `air-temperature` `heat-hazard` `science-communication` `tree-cooling`
    - Sources listed by the author in the comments:
        - "On differences between urban heat signals (#urbanheatislands) for air temperature and LST": [10.1126/sciadv.abb9569](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb9569)
        - "On mismatches between urban humid heat metrics and common LST-based patterns across scales": [10.1029/2022AV000729](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022AV000729)
        - "On discrepancies in cooling efficiency of urban tree cover for LST versus air temperature": [10.1088/1748-9326/ad30a3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ad30a3)
        - "On overestimation of income-based #urbanheat disparities when using LST": [10.1016/j.oneear.2023.05.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oneear.2023.05.016)
        - "Comprehensive critique of using LST as a proxy for urban heat hazard": [Satellite-derived Land Surface Temperatures Strongly Mischaracterise Urban Heat Hazard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16568) — Zhan, Bechtel, Du, Chakraborty, Kotthaus, Krayenhoff, Martilli, Naserikia, Nazarian, Roth, Sismanidis, Stewart & Voogt, preprint submitted 2025-09-20. Argues LST is frequently decoupled from human-relevant thermal impacts, especially during daytime, so using it for adaptation policy inflates estimated benefits.
    - Pushback in the thread worth keeping: Justine Kojo argued the fix is data declaration and stated limitations rather than abandoning LST, since these datasets are genuinely useful in observation-scarce regions when validated against ground data. Chakraborty replied that sparse in-city weather stations are by design (urban signals would contaminate broader climate records), that many research sensor networks do not follow meteorological siting protocols and carry daytime biases, and that city-scale patterns that hold when aggregated across many cities do not necessarily hold for any individual city — so he would be cautious using LST alone for city policy.
