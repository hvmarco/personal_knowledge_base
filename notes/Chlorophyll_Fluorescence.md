# Chlorophyll Fluorescence

**Summary**: Chlorophyll fluorescence as a measurement technique: Fv/Fm and PSII efficiency, quenching analysis, and its use for vitality and stress testing.

## Notes

- **[Growth tracking of basil by proximal remote sensing of chlorophyll fluorescence in growth chamber and greenhouse environments](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2016.08.004)** — Bånkestad & Wik (2016), *Computers and Electronics in Agriculture*. Uses fluorescence rather than reflectance indices to track basil growth continuously, arguing the signal is plant-specific and tied to physiology instead of canopy structure. Keywords: proximal sensing, growth tracking, basil, greenhouse monitoring. Related: [[Greenhouse_Horticulture]], [[Plant_Phenotyping_and_Image_Analysis]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/SVH8REDC) #crop/basil
- **[A Chlorophyll Fluorescence-based Biofeedback System to Control Photosynthetic Lighting in Controlled Environment Agriculture](http://journal.ashspublications.org/content/141/2/169.abstract)** — van Iersel et al. (2016), *Journal of the American Society for Horticultural Science*. Closes the loop between the plant and the fixture: ΦPSII and NPQ drive the lamp output so light is added only while it is still being used photochemically. A direct route to cutting lighting energy. Keywords: biofeedback, ΦPSII, NPQ, lighting control, CEA. Related: [[Lighting_Strategy_and_Control]], [[Photoinhibition_and_Light_Stress]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/9S256AJB)
- **[Chlorophyll fluorescence analysis: a guide to good practice and understanding some new applications](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/ert208)** — Murchie & Lawson (2013), *Journal of Experimental Botany*. The modern methods reference: what each parameter means, how measurement protocol biases it, and where the newer applications are defensible. Keywords: good practice, PSII activity, parameters, protocol. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/22PE5CKU)
- **[A Method for the Rapid Generation of Nonsequential Light-Response Curves of Chlorophyll Fluorescence](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.113.225243)** — Serôdio et al. (2013), *Plant Physiology*. Rapid light curves are contaminated by the light history of the preceding steps; this generates the curve from temporally independent measurements instead, removing that carry-over. Keywords: light-response curves, rapid light curves, hysteresis, method. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/AZZ4J83R)
- **Effects of different light intensities on chlorophyll fluorescence characteristics and yield in lettuce** — Fu et al. (2012), *Scientia Horticulturae*. Tracks how fluorescence parameters and marketable yield in lettuce respond across a range of growth PPFD. Keywords: light intensity, lettuce, yield, fluorescence parameters. Related: [[Lighting_Strategy_and_Control]], [[Greenhouse_Horticulture]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/A58ZWT3K) #crop/lettuce
- **[Chlorophyll fluorescence of stem cambial tissue reflects dormancy development in Juglans nigra seedlings](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11056-012-9334-8)** — Wilson & Jacobs (2012), *New Forests*. Extends fluorescence-based quality assessment to deciduous hardwoods by measuring chlorophyll in stem tissue, which stays available after leaf drop. Keywords: stem fluorescence, dormancy, hardwood seedlings, quality assessment. Related: [[Photoperiod_and_Dormancy_Induction]], [[Forest_Seedling_Production]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/9XIB2WAY)
- **[When it is too hot for photosynthesis: heat-induced instability of photosynthesis in relation to respiratory burst, cell permeability changes and H2O2 formation](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.2010.02229.x)** — Hüve et al. (2011), *Plant, Cell & Environment*. Locates the critical temperature for the F0 rise at 46–48 °C in bean and ties the loss of photosynthetic stability to a respiratory burst, membrane leakage and H2O2 — so the fluorescence signal marks real cellular damage, not just a transient. Keywords: heat stress, F0 rise, critical temperature, membrane permeability. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]], [[Photoinhibition_and_Light_Stress]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/85DUS7F8) #project/thesis #project/msc-thesis
- **[Canopy uptake of atmospheric N deposition at a conifer forest: response of chlorophyll fluorescence and gas exchange parameters](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0889.2007.00265.x)** — Tomaszewski & Sievering (2007), *Tellus B*. Sprays spruce foliage with ammonium nitrate at 60% above ambient wet deposition and reads the response in fluorescence and gas exchange — canopy N uptake shows up as measurable photosynthetic change. Keywords: nitrogen deposition, canopy uptake, spruce, gas exchange. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/YJWWRAH9)
- **[Chlorophyll fluorescence characteristics, performance and survival of freshly lifted and cold stored Douglas fir seedlings](https://doi.org/10.1051/forest:2001122)** — Perks et al. (2001), *Annals of Forest Science*. Follows fluorescence through lifting and cold storage in Douglas fir and relates it to subsequent field performance and survival. Keywords: cold storage, lifting date, Douglas fir, survival. Related: [[Frost_Hardiness_and_Freezing_Tests]], [[Forest_Seedling_Production]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/U5SEMU4T)
- **[Chlorophyll fluorescence — a practical guide](https://doi.org/10.1093/jexbot/51.345.659)** — Maxwell & Johnson (2000), *Journal of Experimental Botany*. The standard introduction: enough theory to read the parameters correctly, the technical traps, and worked examples of what fluorescence can and cannot tell you. Start here. Keywords: introduction, methodology, parameters, pitfalls. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/QQ4KEZ6L)
- **[Evaluation of instant light-response curves of chlorophyll fluorescence parameters obtained with a portable chlorophyll fluorometer on site in the field](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-3040.2000.00650.x)** — Rascher et al. (2000), *Plant, Cell & Environment*. Tests whether the built-in fast light-curve programmes on portable PAM instruments give meaningful numbers when photosynthesis is nowhere near steady state. Keywords: PAM fluorometry, light curves, steady state, field measurement. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/2ZWCE5TM)
- **[Applications of chlorophyll fluorescence for stock quality assessment with different types of fluorometers](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1006513720073)** — Binder et al. (1997), *New Forests*. Compares a laboratory integrating fluorometer against two field-portable pulse-modulated instruments for detecting freezing, drought and heat damage in conifer stock, with r² > 0.90 against visible injury. Keywords: fluorometer comparison, stock quality, freezing damage, portable instruments. Related: [[Forest_Seedling_Production]], [[Frost_Hardiness_and_Freezing_Tests]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/2U5BULI7)
- **[Chlorophyll fluorescence as an indicator of frost hardiness in white spruce seedlings from different latitudes](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00036784)** — Binder & Fielder (1996), *New Forests*. Variable fluorescence predicts visible freezing damage well in September (r² up to 0.99) but fails from October onward, once test temperatures no longer exceed the seedlings' hardiness. A clear statement of the method's seasonal window. Keywords: frost hardiness, variable fluorescence, white spruce, seasonal limits. Related: [[Frost_Hardiness_and_Freezing_Tests]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/USXHANKL)
- **[Determining photosynthetic parameters from leaf CO2 exchange and chlorophyll fluorescence](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC157790/)** — Laisk & Loreto (1996), *Plant Physiology*. Combines simultaneous gas exchange and fluorescence to extract Rubisco CO2/O2 specificity, excitation partitioning to PSII, day respiration, alternative electron transport and mesophyll transport resistance for single leaves. Keywords: Rubisco specificity, mesophyll conductance, electron transport, combined measurement. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/YLMDMJQC)
- **Chlorophyll fluorescence: a review of its practical forestry applications and instrumentation** — Mohammed et al. (1995), *Scandinavian Journal of Forest Research*. Survey of how fluorescence had been put to work in forestry practice, and of the instruments available at the time. Keywords: forestry applications, instrumentation, review. Related: [[Forest_Seedling_Production]], [[Photoperiod_and_Dormancy_Induction]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/BNA9E9DY)
- **[A manual for the use of variable chlorophyll fluorescence in the assessment of the ecophysiology of conifer seedlings](https://cfs.nrcan.gc.ca/publications?id=3655)** — Vidaver et al. (1991), *Canadian Forest Service*. The practical nursery manual: how to run the measurement on conifer seedlings and how to read the result for stress and dormancy status. Keywords: manual, conifer seedlings, ecophysiology, protocol. Related: [[Forest_Seedling_Production]], [[Frost_Hardiness_and_Freezing_Tests]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/NDPSVVUU)
- **[The relationship between the quantum yield of photosynthetic electron transport and quenching of chlorophyll fluorescence](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4165(89)80016-9)** — Genty et al. (1989), *Biochimica et Biophysica Acta*. The paper behind ΦPSII: across species and conditions the quantum yield of non-cyclic electron transport is proportional to qP × Fv'/Fm', giving the single parameter ΔF/Fm′ that fluorescence work has used ever since. Keywords: ΦPSII, quenching, electron transport, Genty parameter. Related: [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]]. [zotero](zotero://select/library/items/689JMR7K)
- **[Photon yield of O2 evolution and chlorophyll fluorescence characteristics at 77 K among vascular plants of diverse origins](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00402983)** — Björkman & Demmig (1987), *Planta*. Establishes that unstressed C3 plants share a near-constant photon yield (0.106 O2 per absorbed photon across 37 species) and fixes the Fv/Fm ≈ 0.83 benchmark that stress work is measured against. Keywords: photon yield, Fv/Fm baseline, C3 and C4, 77 K fluorescence. Related: [[Photoinhibition_and_Light_Stress]], [[Photosynthesis_and_Gas_Exchange]].
