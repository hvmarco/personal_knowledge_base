The northern hemisphere [#summer](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23summer&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) is underway, & with it, expectedly, we have a new crop of record-breaking temperatures & [#heatwaves](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23heatwaves&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) in various parts of the world ([#climatechange](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23climatechange&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) et al.)

And with discussions of [#extremeheat](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23extremeheat&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) come, every single year, maps of [#satellite](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23satellite&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED)\-derived [#landsurfacetemperature](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23landsurfacetemperature&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) (LST). These maps show massive [#temperature](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23temperature&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) values compared to the [#airtemperature](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23airtemperature&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) used in [#weather](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23weather&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) reports. LST maps also show extreme differences across regions & land cover types, especially within cities. And social media is filled with these "55°C in Madrid; 27°C in a nearby forest" type posts; not just from random engagement farmers, but also from so-called authoritative sources.

The problem is that LST values are almost never equivalent to the temperature of the air around us. LST represents the bulk radiometric "skin" temperature of whatever a satellite sees, which, in [#urban](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23urban&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) settings, includes an amalgamation of rooftops, tops of tree canopies, asphalt roads, parking lots, etc. As surfaces absorb solar radiation throughout the daytime, they get much warmer than the air above them. But unless you're walking barefoot on that surface, that's not the magnitude of heat you feel. We interact with the air around us, which is almost always much cooler than most surfaces during daytime. And while LST (rather, skin temperature) does modulate emitted thermal radiation from the ground, remember that there is a huge difference in heat when touching a heated pan versus hovering your hand near it.

Using LST also overstates the benefits of [#trees](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23trees&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) on air temperature. Trees provide critical shade, which drastically lowers the LST of the ground right underneath them. But when it comes to reducing air temperature during the day, that reduction is an order of magnitude smaller (& shading benefits are hyper-local + these posts rarely focus on heat metrics that explicitly account for radiation). Furthermore, when we use satellites to estimate cooling efficiency of trees, we don't even see the ground underneath the tree. We are basically comparing the LST of tops of tree canopies, which are actively transpiring, against heated [#asphalt](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23asphalt&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) roads & [#parkinglots](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23parkinglots&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED).

Many of these posts add disclaimers like "this is surface temperature, not air temperature, but..." & then continue talking about those massive numbers, creating an anchoring effect. It shocks people into overestimating the heat hazard. More importantly, when people's lived experience of outdoor conditions doesn't match what they assume 50°C might feel like, it reduces trust in the data & the science, which is an ongoing concern in this area.

This is not a new issue. It's been happening for years as satellite data have become easier to access & visualize, not just in public-facing media, but also in scientific papers.

Basically, using LST to quantify [#heat](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23heat&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) hazard is actively misleading those who are not scientifically trained to understand the nuances in that variable.

Please stop.

> > [Justine Kojo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/justine-kojo/) A couple of things: 1. While it is true that there are less observations overall in Africa, that there are less weather stations to capture useful spatial information within cities is not specific to Africa. It is by design, because we do not want urban signals to contaminate broader weather and climate signals. While there are many cities in developed countries with more 'urban' air temp measurements, these are not operational weather stations, but networks set up for research purposes. A broader problem here is that many of these sensors do not follow the protocols for meteorological observations, which can lead to substantial biases (especially during daytime). 2. Regarding the use of LST, I think I clearly separated out the qualitative versus quantitative uses (let me know what specifically you have an issue with, especially regarding what we said in the preprint). However, even for qualitative use cases, while we see broad-scale similarities in patterns for air temperature and LST when aggregated across cities, that does not automatically mean that you would see that for an individual city. As such, if the goal is policy and action for a city, I would be cautious of using LST solely for those purposes.
